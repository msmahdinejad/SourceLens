# Charge While You Shop: Evidence Brief

# **How EV Fast Charging at Stores Turns an Everyday Shopping Stop into Useful Road-Trip Time**
*A guide for drivers, retailers, and charging planners*

## **Executive Summary**
Electric vehicle (EV) fast charging at retail stores is transforming the way drivers power their cars—turning a routine shopping trip into productive road-trip time. For drivers, it means less waiting and more convenience. For retailers, it attracts customers who stay longer and spend more. For charging planners, it helps meet growing demand while optimizing grid impact.

This report explains:
- **Why drivers prefer fast charging** at stores during long trips or quick errands.
- **How retailers benefit** from increased foot traffic and customer loyalty.
- **What planners must consider** to ensure reliable, accessible, and grid-friendly charging.

Key takeaways:
✔ **Fast charging (DC) is preferred for road trips**—drivers want to minimize wait times [S1].
✔ **Retailers like Walmart and Ricker’s** see higher customer engagement when offering charging [S3][S12].
✔ **Planners must balance speed, cost, and grid impact**—higher-power chargers (150–350 kW) are in demand, but battery limits and grid capacity matter [S4][S11].
✔ **Convenience is critical**—stores within 10 miles of most Americans make charging accessible [S10].

---

## **1. Why Drivers Love Fast Charging at Stores**
### **1.1 Fast Charging Saves Time on Road Trips**
- **DC fast charging (50–350 kW) is 3–10x faster than AC charging** [S1].
- Drivers on long trips prefer fast charging to **reduce wait times**—especially during holidays when charging stations are crowded [S1].
- **Example:** Tesla’s Supercharger network averages **27.5 minutes per session** (20%–80% charge) [S18].

### **1.2 Shopping While Charging = No Wasted Time**
- **Retailers like Walmart and Sam’s Club** place chargers near stores, letting drivers **grab groceries, eat, or shop** while their car powers up [S3][S10].
- **Ricker’s convenience stores** saw **steady customer growth** after installing fast chargers—drivers stayed longer and spent more [S5][S12].
- **Tesla’s Destination Charging** partners with hotels, restaurants, and stores to offer **convenient, on-site charging** [S14].

### **1.3 Range Anxiety? Fast Charging Helps**
- **Range anxiety** (fear of running out of power) is a top concern—especially in winter when heating drains batteries [S1].
- **Fast chargers every 10–15 miles** (like Nissan’s network in Indianapolis) help drivers **recharge quickly** and keep moving [S19].
- **Walmart’s plan to install thousands of fast chargers** will make EV ownership easier for rural and urban drivers alike [S10].

---

## **2. How Retailers Benefit from Offering Fast Charging**
### **2.1 More Customers = More Sales**
- **EV drivers spend 20–30 minutes charging**—enough time to **shop, eat, or browse** [S3][S10].
- **Ricker’s convenience stores** saw **increased foot traffic** after installing chargers, with drivers buying snacks, drinks, and other items [S5].
- **Walmart’s "Every Day Low Price" charging** makes EV ownership more affordable, attracting cost-conscious shoppers [S10].

### **2.2 Low-Cost Entry with Big Returns**
- **Nissan’s "No Charge to Charge" program** provided **free chargers to retailers**, reducing upfront costs [S12].
- **Ricker’s spent only $2,000–$3,000 per site** for installation, with **no major challenges** [S19].
- **Government incentives** (like Clean Cities Coalitions) help retailers **offset costs** [S5].

### **2.3 Future-Proofing for EV Growth**
- **By 2030, Walmart plans to install fast chargers at thousands of U.S. locations**—positioning itself as a leader in EV infrastructure [S10].
- **Retailers that adopt charging early** gain a **competitive edge** as EV adoption grows [S12].

---

## **3. What Charging Planners Need to Know**
### **3.1 Fast Charging Demand is Rising—But Battery Limits Matter**
- **Drivers prefer the fastest charging their car can handle** (up to 350 kW) [S11].
- **Battery technology limits charging speed**—most EVs can’t sustain max power for long [S4].
- **Example:** A 350 kW charger may only deliver **150–250 kW** due to battery thermal limits [S18].

### **3.2 Grid Impact: Balancing Speed and Stability**
- **High-power chargers (150–350 kW) strain local grids**—planners must assess capacity [S2].
- **Smart charging (V2G, load balancing) can help**—EVs can **store and return energy** to the grid [S2][S22].
- **Modular designs allow upgrades**—future-proofing stations for higher power [S9].

### **3.3 Location Matters: Where to Place Chargers**
- **Retail stores, highways, and urban hubs** are ideal—drivers want **convenience** [S13].
- **Walmart’s 10-mile coverage** ensures **90% of Americans** have access [S10].
- **Case study:** Ricker’s placed chargers at **56 convenience stores** across Indianapolis for **maximum reach** [S5].

### **3.4 Cost vs. Speed: Finding the Right Balance**
| **Charger Type** | **Power (kW)** | **Best For** | **Cost Considerations** |
|-----------------|--------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **AC (Level 2)** | 7–22 kW | Home, work, overnight | Low cost, slow charging [S1] |
| **DC Fast (50 kW)** | 50 kW | Convenience stores, short stops | Affordable, but slower [S19] |
| **DC Fast (150–350 kW)** | 150–350 kW | Highways, road trips | Expensive, but fastest [S11] |

- **Retailers often start with 50 kW chargers** (like Ricker’s) before upgrading [S19].
- **Walmart’s network will include high-power chargers** to meet future demand [S10].

---

## **4. Recommendations**
### **For Drivers:**
✅ **Plan charging stops at stores**—combine errands with charging to save time.
✅ **Use fast chargers (DC) for road trips**—AC charging is better for overnight or long dwells.
✅ **Check charging networks** (Tesla, Electrify America, Walmart) for **convenient locations**.

### **For Retailers:**
🏪 **Start with fast chargers (50–150 kW)**—low-cost entry with high customer appeal.
💡 **Partner with automakers or government programs** (like Nissan’s "No Charge to Charge") to **reduce costs**.
📈 **Track customer behavior**—see how charging impacts sales and foot traffic.

### **For Charging Planners:**
⚡ **Prioritize high-traffic retail locations**—stores, highways, and urban centers.
🔌 **Plan for grid upgrades**—high-power chargers need **smart load management**.
📊 **Use data to optimize placement**—tools like **EVI-Pro** help predict demand [S18].

---

## **5. Conclusion**
EV fast charging at stores is a **win-win-win**:
- **Drivers** get **faster, more convenient charging** while shopping.
- **Retailers** attract **more customers** who stay longer and spend more.
- **Planners** can **meet demand** while optimizing grid impact.

As EV adoption grows, **retail charging will play a key role** in making electric driving **easier, faster, and more accessible** for everyone.
