# Electric-mobility demo source and licensing research

Research date: 2026-08-09

## Recommended demo story

**Charge While You Shop: how EV fast charging is turning the retail stop into part of the road trip.**
**شارژ هنگام خرید: چگونه شارژ سریع، توقفگاه‌های خرده‌فروشی را به بخشی از سفر برقی تبدیل می‌کند.**

The central question is deliberately human rather than purely technical: **Can a supermarket or
shopping-centre car park become a convenient, reliable, interoperable, and grid-aware charging
stop without overselling what the evidence proves?** The story joins cars, recognizable companies,
retail destinations, consumer preferences, charging standards, and the electricity grid. It can be
understood without specialist knowledge, yet it still supports serious evidence comparison.

The visual arc is unusually strong: an electric car on the road, a connector close-up, a driver
plugging in, a retail car park, shopping while the car charges, a fast-charging hub, and a
solar/grid transition. Those scenes map naturally to real Pexels inventory and avoid filler in
slides or video. The source boundary also creates a useful critical-thinking exercise: peer-reviewed
and public-sector evidence must be distinguished from Walmart and Tesla's own product claims.

## Official sources to add through Search (five)

These should be added as linked/search-derived sources, not vendored as redistributable files unless their individual rights are checked separately.

1. **International Energy Agency — [Global EV Outlook 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026):** current global EV and charging context. The report page labels the publication CC BY 4.0, but IEA [excludes logos, photos, third-party material, and standalone datasets](https://www.iea.org/terms/creative-commons-cc-licenses); import the text page through Search and do not vendor its cover or photos.
2. **U.S. Department of Energy AFDC — [Charging Electric Vehicles in Public](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-public):** a neutral explanation of Level 2 and DC fast charging and why shopping centres, hotels, workplaces, corridors, and hubs have different dwell-time needs.
3. **Joint Office of Energy and Transportation — [Public EV Charging Infrastructure Playbook](https://driveelectric.gov/ev-infrastructure-playbook):** official planning modules for engagement, siting, funding, procurement, revenue, accessibility, and retail site hosts.
4. **Walmart — [Leading the Charge](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2023/04/06/leading-the-charge-walmart-announces-plan-to-expand-electric-vehicle-charging-network):** the retailer's 2023 plan for a fast-charging network at thousands of Walmart and Sam's Club locations. Store counts, coverage, convenience, and price language are **Walmart claims/intentions**, not independently verified outcomes.
5. **Tesla — [Supercharging](https://www.tesla.com/support/charging/supercharging):** the manufacturer's current description of route planning, charging workflow, payment, availability, amenities, and vehicle/network limitations. Speed and network-size figures must be labeled **Tesla claims** and dated at retrieval.

Together these sources give the demo market context, human preferences, public implementation
guidance, a retail strategy, and a branded charging experience. Search imports should retain URL,
publisher, retrieval date, and page title. Corporate text and images are not bundled in the public
repository; generated outputs may summarize cited text but must not reproduce company logos,
screenshots, or promotional imagery unless separately licensed.

### Pexels rights and API obligations

The [Pexels license](https://www.pexels.com/license/) allows Pexels photos and videos to be used for free and modified; ordinary media use does not require attribution, although credit is encouraged. It prohibits selling unaltered copies, implying endorsement, portraying identifiable people offensively, redistributing assets on another stock/wallpaper service, and using media as a trademark or service mark.

The [Pexels API documentation](https://www.pexels.com/api/documentation/) adds product-facing requirements: show a prominent link to Pexels whenever the API is used, credit creators when possible with a link to the media page, do not replicate Pexels’ core stock-library functionality, and respect API rate limits. Therefore SourceLens should persist `creator`, `creator_url`, `pexels_page_url`, and asset ID in provenance; exports should include a compact media-credit section even though the general media license does not make attribution mandatory.

### Residual media risk

Pexels’ license does not guarantee that every depicted trademark, property, or person is cleared for every imaginable use. Avoid visible vehicle logos when practical, do not suggest that a depicted person or manufacturer endorses SourceLens, and prefer shots centered on infrastructure, cables, connectors, dashboards, streetscapes, and vehicles without identifiable people.

### Evidence and rights checks for the live run

- Preserve title, authors/issuer, DOI or canonical URL, license, retrieval date, and selected source IDs.
- Attribute both seed PDFs under CC BY 4.0 and state modifications in translated or derived outputs.
- Treat IEA, Walmart, and Tesla numbers according to their own scope and retrieval date; do not turn a target or product maximum into an observed universal outcome.
